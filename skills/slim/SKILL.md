# Slim

An AI writes down everything it knows, and every line is correct — which is why nothing got cut. The reader pays for every line anyway. Correct is not the same as worth keeping.

Delete first, rewrite second. Slim whatever the user points at.

## Name the reader

Who opens this, and what are they doing? "Needed" means nothing until that is settled.

## The test

Delete a sentence, a table row, a section. Does the reader now get stuck, decide wrong, or have to go ask someone? If not, it stays deleted.

Structure counts too: when every line in a section fails, the heading goes with them. And moving text isn't cutting it — a fact relocated to the bottom still costs the reader.

What usually fails is whatever the author found interesting rather than what the reader needs: preamble, the same fact restated, explanation the subject already gives, completeness for its own sake, speculation about what might come later, and general truths that would hold in any project.

What usually passes is what the reader can't recover alone: the trap that isn't visible from the code, and the one line of *why* behind a choice. That second one is what over-eager cutting destroys first.

Borderline lines stay. One missing line costs the reader more than one spare.

## Then compress, then check

Tighten what survived without changing its language, voice, or claims.

Read it through. Cuts leave seams — a reference to a section that's gone, a step assuming something now unstated. Fix them.

Report in chat what you removed, flagging anything the reader might want back. The document says nothing about having been slimmed.
